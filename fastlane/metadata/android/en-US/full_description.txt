An Android app is offline exactly when its manifest lacks the INTERNET permission — without it, the operating system blocks every network connection the app could open. Offline Checker shows you which apps hold that permission, and how much data the ones that do send and receive.

• Lists visible installed apps and marks each one. Apps with INTERNET show received and sent traffic for the last 24 hours or 7 days (7 days by default). Apps without it are marked "No INTERNET permission" — a fact read straight from the manifest, not a vendor's word.
• The default sort puts the apps that reach the internet at the top, ordered by total traffic, then the rest by name. A sort badge opens a Usage/Name dropdown; Name sorts every app alphabetically.
• System apps are hidden by default; a top-bar toggle includes them.
• Traffic totals are read on-device from Android's own per-app network statistics — the same accounting the system's Data usage screen shows in aggregate.

Offline Checker itself has no INTERNET permission: Android blocks every network call it could ever make. It also deliberately does not request QUERY_ALL_PACKAGES, the Play-restricted permission that lifts Android 11's package-visibility filter. Its one special grant is Usage access, which Android requires before an app may read other apps' network statistics; the app's button takes you to that grant screen.

There are no ads, no libraries, no trackers — one Activity, plain platform views, MIT-licensed source you can audit.

Limitations: Android exposes per-app byte totals only, not destinations — seeing domains or IPs requires a local VPN tool. Without QUERY_ALL_PACKAGES, some non-launchable background apps are hidden by package visibility and cannot be listed. Apps sharing a UID are counted together, and some devices keep less than 7 days of network history.
